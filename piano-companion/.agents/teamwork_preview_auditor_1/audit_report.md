## Phase 2: Domain-Specific Integrity & Authenticity Verifications

### 2.1 Authenticity of Persian Linguistics, Phonology & Orthography
The deliverable was subjected to specialist linguistic review to verify that Persian terms, transliterations, and phonetic concepts are genuine:

1. **Root Morphemes**:
   - `نما` (*Nama*): Correctly identified as the authentic Persian root for visual screen, showcase, panorama, display (e.g. *Filmnama*, *Namava*, *Rahnama*).
   - `نوا` (*Nava*): Authentic Persian musical root for melody, song, tune, and the name of one of the seven classical *Dastgāhs*.
   - `ساز` (*Saz*): Authentic Persian root for musical instrument / music making.
   - `آهنگ` (*Ahang*): Authentic everyday Persian word for song, melody, rhythm.
   - `ترانه` (*Taraneh*): Authentic Persian word for ballad, lyric, song.
   - `کلاویه` (*Klāviyeh*): Authentic formal Persian loanword (from German/French *Klavier*) used by Iranian pianists and conservatories for piano keys.
2. **Phonotactics & Syllable Constraints**:
   - The analysis of the Persian `#CC` onset cluster barrier is linguistically accurate. Persian syllable structure strictly forbids initial consonant clusters (`*#CC`). Foreign words such as *Stream* `/striːm/` undergo prothetic vowel epenthesis, becoming `[ʔes.triːm]`.
   - Compounding *Piano* + *Stream* legitimately results in 5 syllables (`pi-yâ-no-es-trīm`), whereas *Piano* + *Nama* maintains pure `CV` alternating syllables (`pi-yâ-no-na-mâ`), which is vastly superior for child mouthfeel.
3. **Perso-Arabic Orthography & Ligature Connectivity**:
   - The document notes that the letter *Vav* (و) in `پیانو` is an inherently non-joining letter on the left side.
   - Forensic check: In Arabic and Persian calligraphy, the set of non-joining letters consists of `ا` (alef), `د` (dal), `ذ` (zal), `ر` (re), `ز` (ze), `ژ` (zhe), and `و` (vav). Because *Vav* never joins to the left, `پیانو` + `نما` writes as **پیانونما**, which renders identically whether typed with a ZWNJ (`U+200C`), without a ZWNJ, or with a space. This eliminates search fragmentation in app stores.
4. **Cross-Linguistic Taboo & Slang Screening**:
   - *Pip* (پیپ): In contemporary Persian, "pip" refers specifically to a tobacco smoking pipe (`pip keshidan`). Elevating *Pip* to the app marquee (*PipKeys*, *PipPiano*) would indeed cause an awkward schoolyard association in Iranian families. Isolating Pip as the in-app mascot guide (`Pip the Songbird` / «پیپ، پرنده آوازخوان») is culturally sound.
   - *Goh* (گوه / گه): Standard Persian vulgar term for excrement. The veto against "Go" compounds (e.g. *GoPiano*, *PianoGo*) is valid.
   - *Kos* (کوس / کس) & *Kir* (کیر): Severe vulgar terms in Persian. The warning that children eliding final /t/ in *-cast* (*PianoCast* -> *Piano-kas*) could trigger acoustic collisions is linguistically plausible and demonstrates rigorous defensive naming.
5. **Persian Taglines & Grammar**:
   - «ببین، بنواز، بدرخش!» (*Bebin, Benavâz, Bederekhsh!*): Perfect 2nd-person singular imperative verb harmony (Watch, Play, Shine!).
   - «تماشاخانه ویدیویی پیانو برای کودکان»: Grammatically flawless noun phrase ("The Video Theater of Piano for Children").
   - «هر ویدیو یک آهنگ، هر کلید یک لبخند!»: Poetic, child-friendly parallel phrasing ("Every video a song, every key a smile!").
   - «جایی که انگشتان کوچک، ملودی‌های بزرگ می‌سازند.»: Natural, elegant relative clause.
   - In-app micro-copy strings (welcome banner, looper encouragement, 3-star celebration) are grammatically natural and culturally warm.
